## 1. Stages and thirst caps

"Thirst" is how much need and pressure a message puts on the other person, 0-5. Each stage has a cap:

| # | Stage | Thirst cap | Tone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Just met | 0 | Friendly and interesting; the goal is "fun to talk to", not "into you". Match their reply length. |
| 1 | Talking stage | 1.5 | Warm, playful, a little ambiguous; get to a first date fast — the talking stage is where things go to die. |
| 2 | Dating | 2 | Clear interest is fine; keep dates happening. Not exclusive by default. |
| 3 | About to DTR | 2.5 | Say it clearly and calmly, like something obvious; if they need time, give it without pushing. |
| 4 | Honeymoon | 3.5 | Sweet is fine; keep your own life and some surprise. |
| 5 | Steady | 3 | Comfortable beats performative; keep it fresh with small surprises. |
| 6 | Rough patch | 1.5 | Cool it down; talk about how you feel ("I felt kind of brushed off") rather than what they always do. |
| 7 | On the rocks | 0.5 | Calm and dignified; no apology floods, no begging; say the important thing short. |

Stages move on actions, not vibes (reading.md): **great texting doesn't upgrade the stage; time, plans and choices do.**

Early on (0-2): the fastest way to kill it is to over-invest before they have. Keep the ratio even — if they send one text, don't send four. Plans beat texting marathons. When they go dry twice, you don't turn up the charm, you pull back and see.

## 2. How to score thirst

| Score | Feels like | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | No romantic signal | "is that place busy on weekends" |
| 1 | Barely there | "you seem a bit off today" |
| 2 | Clear interest, not heavy | "ok I'm enjoying this way too much" |
| 3 | Direct feelings | "I miss you" |
| 4 | High intensity | "babe how was your day ❤️" |
| 5 | Pressure | "why aren't you answering me, I've been waiting" |

**Scoring procedure (no grading yourself easy)**: first describe in one phrase how much need it shows and how much pressure it puts on them, then score from that description. When unsure, score higher. Over the cap? Rewrite the move; don't shave the number.

Hidden thirst (+1 each): waiting energy ("finally you reply"), reassurance-seeking ("are you mad at me?"), asking the same thing twice, "ok" followed by "cool well I'll let you go then", replying 3x their length, a trailing "?" that isn't a question.

Anti-thirst = keeping attention on your own life: mention what you're doing while you talk, end the conversation yourself sometimes, share your status instead of checking theirs.

## 3. Pacing

**Nobody's impressed by strategic delays anymore.** The "wait three hours so you don't look eager" era reads as games in 2026, and people are openly tired of games. Pacing here is about matching energy and not over-investing — not about fake unavailability.

Moves: **leave a hook** (say half, leave a thread for them to pull), **hand the ball back** (they're flirting but not planning — "ok so when"), **show your life** (share something you're doing), **step back** (they've gone dry — give it room and see), **close the loop** (end the conversation on a high note), **ask them out** (it's warm — give a specific low-pressure plan).

| Situation | Reply timing |
| --- | --- |
| They're engaged and it's flowing | Reply when you see it — don't disappear mid-flow |
| Normal day-to-day | Whenever you naturally see it; no need to rush |
| They're flirting but not planning | Reply, and hand the ball back with a specific ask |
| They're dry (one-word answers) | Match it; don't write paragraphs to a "lol" |
| They warmed back up after going cold | Receive it warmly, don't overcompensate |
| Lots of talk, no plans | Hand the ball back once, then wait for them to name a time |
| User has reached out 2-3 times in a row | Stop for a few days; let them come to you |

Give a clear call on going quiet: no / short pause / close the loop / step back for a few days. Never go quiet while it's flowing or right after they sent something great; once they've named a time and place, just make the plan.

For moves involving pacing, pushing forward, cooling down, or low interest, add a strategy block (format in core.md).

**Game-playing warning**: hot-and-cold, fake busyness and "making them chase" mostly make people tired, and they leave. With direct, slow-burn, or serious-about-dating people, games break something that was working.

## 4. Pacing intensity 0-3

| Level | Name | How | Fits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Just match | Reply naturally, engage fully, no pacing games | Clearly into you, slow burners, direct people |
| 1 | Light | Leave hooks, hand the ball back, end on a high note | Normal flirting, plays along but doesn't initiate much |
| 2 | Step back | Close loops, let them initiate the next one | App-popular, social butterflies, hot-and-cold |
| 3 | Pause | Stop initiating for a few days, only reward real effort, action tests | Player signals, all talk no plans, user's been chasing |

Level 2-3 needs at least two of: they're used to attention and immune to effort / they flirt but won't plan / the user's been carrying it and they're giving short replies / they warm up when the user steps back / player score 41+. Execution: reward real effort normally, close the loop on low effort, hand the ball back on plan-less flirting, don't escalate when they suddenly warm up.

Protect from pacing games (hard pacing breaks these): direct people, slow burners, people clearly looking for something serious, sensitive people, anyone who has already named a time or keeps initiating. With them: match, stay low-thirst warm, and make specific plans.

## 5. Pursuit types

Read the chat first, then their socials. Use gender scripts only when the pattern really shows up; update with outcomes.

**Dating women — common types**:

| Type | Visible evidence | Main move | Pacing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Aesthetic / curated | Styled feed, cafés, galleries, outfits | Compliment a specific taste; plan a date with some thought in it | 1-2 |
| Deep feeler | Long captions, lyrics, late-night thoughts | Meet the feeling first, then show you're steady | 0-1 |
| Social butterfly | Always out, busy comments | Show your own life; invite her to something with energy | 1-2 |
| Slow burner | Rarely posts, slow but real replies | Lower the frequency, win with details | 0-1 |
| Career-focused | Work, gym, goals | Short and substantive; efficient, well-planned asks | 0-1 |
| App-popular / high options | Lots of attention, flirty but few plans | Don't over-feed attention; hand the ball back; action test | 2-3 |

Mistakes: interviewing her; putting her on a pedestal too early; only complimenting and never showing yourself; great chats that never become a date; a vague "we should hang out sometime" instead of a real ask.

**Dating men — common types**:

| Type | Visible evidence | Main move | Pacing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Direct / literal | Doesn't pick up hints, plain steady replies | Be clear — he won't decode subtle hints | 0-1 |
| Practical / busy | Goal-driven, short logical texts | Keep it light and efficient; fewer emotional essays | 0-1 |
| Banter guy | Memes, roasting, games, sports | Challenge, light roasting, inside jokes | 1-2 |
| Slow / passive | Replies but rarely starts | Make it easy: a clear low-cost invite | 0-1 |
| High options / confident | Used to attention | Less hype, more selective; specific compliments | 2 |
| Player | Smooth talker, late-night, no plans | Ignore words, watch follow-through; step back | 2-3 |

Mistakes: dropping hints and expecting them decoded; playing too cool and missing the window; reading "short and direct" as "not interested"; giving a player too much emotional attention and becoming a backup option.

Women asking men out is normal and usually welcome; apps where women message first make this standard. Whoever's more into it can make the plan.

When relevant ("Any chance?", social profiles, complex Replies), note in the commentary: **Pursuit type** · pacing 0-3, **why** (two pieces of evidence), **play** (show your life / light flirt / step back / hand the ball back / action test).

## 6. Vibe channels: playful vs warm

Not a binary, a dial. The same message in a different vibe lands twice as well or half as well.

| | Playful / cheeky | Warm / steady |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Feels like | Own rhythm, doesn't give it all away, a bit of challenge | Reliable, safe, attentive |
| Moves | Teasing, leaving things unsaid, confident jokes, not always instant | Real engagement, remembering details, making plans, steady mood |
| Flirting | Understated, contrast, a little mystery | Sincere and direct, care and presence |
| Fails as | Cocky, try-hard, exhausting hot-and-cold | Clingy, boring, "nice guy/girl" |
| Who likes it | People with options, easily bored, like a challenge, a little rebellious | People who value security, have been burned, slow burners |

Read which one they respond to: they roast you, are immune to over-attentiveness, like a challenge → playful. Slow to warm, get nervous when flirted with, care whether you remember things, withdraw when you play it cool → warm. Many people want both in different moments (reliable about plans, cheeky in banter) — say which moment gets which. Default to warm when unsure.

**Only amplify what the user actually has.** If they never tease, don't hand them roasts — give the light version (a shorter reply, a bit of mystery). Playful doesn't mean higher thirst; warm doesn't mean needy. The thirst cap always wins.

Same intent, two vibes (they say "today was so long"):

- Playful: `and you still replied to me, I'm honored`
- Warm: `that sounds rough` + `do nothing tonight, you earned it`

They reappear after going quiet:

- Playful: `oh look who's alive`
- Warm: skip it, open fresh: `saw this and thought of you`

## 7. Personality base

A slower layer than stage or interest; it explains why they react how they do. Only fill in with evidence; mark unknowns "not sure yet", and don't fill it from star signs or MBTI.

| Axis | Ends | How you see it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Social energy | Introvert, one-on-one ↔ extrovert, recharges in groups | How busy their socials are; quiet vs lively date spots |
| Stability ↔ novelty | Routine ↔ loves trying new things | How they react to new plans and last-minute changes |
| Logic ↔ feeling | Wants solutions ↔ wants to be heard | When they vent, do they want advice or empathy |
| Planner ↔ spontaneous | Books ahead ↔ decides on the day | Whether dates need details locked in |
| Rules ↔ edge | Plays it straight ↔ likes a little rebellion | Decides playful vs warm (section 6) |
| Says it ↔ holds it in | Direct ↔ wants to be read | How much subtext they use |
| Security source | Control / being cared for / space / being valued | When they relax, when they overthink |

Keep lists in the dossier: **likes** (what lights them up), **dislikes**, **wishlist** (small things they've said they want to try — the best date material, see dating.md). Personality sets the base tone: logical people get fewer emotional essays; feeling-first people get empathy first. Security-seekers don't get pacing games. One contradicting message is a lead; two or three consistent ones change the read.

## 8. Line library and fixing borrowed lines

Every line here is a skeleton; run it through voice.md, the thirst cap, vibe match and "sounds like the user" before using it. Tags: `[vibe thirst]`.

**Openers**
- `[any 0.5]` Off a specific detail in their profile or story: `ok where is this place, it looks unreal`
- `[any 1]` Story reply: `this playlist is dangerously good`
- `[playful 1]` Hinge-prompt callback: `bold of you to say you make the best pasta, I'll need proof`
- `[warm 0.5]` `saw this and thought of what you said about your dog`

**Comfort (they're tired / down)**
- `[any 1]` `that sounds exhausting` + `takeout and zero plans tonight`
- `[playful 1]` `and you still replied to me, I'm honored`
- `[warm 1]` `do nothing tonight` + `vent if you want, I'm around`

**Flirting (watch the cap)**
- `[any 1]` `ok I'm enjoying this conversation more than I planned to`
- `[playful 1.5]` `you're a lot of trouble, lucky I'm patient`
- `[warm 1]` `how did it get this late already`
- Close with a hook: `gotta go, part two tomorrow`

**Asking out (specific, not "sometime")**
- `[any 1]` `let me take you for a drink thursday`
- `[any 1]` `there's a pop-up gallery saturday afternoon, come with?`
- `[warm 1]` `I looked up that ramen place you mentioned, want to go this weekend?`
- Deflected without a new time: `no worries, let me know when you're free` — and then let them lead.

**Reconnecting**
- After they vanish: `[playful 1]` `oh look who's alive`
- All talk, no plan: `[any 1]` `ok so when`
- Show your life: `[any 0.5]` `just did the hike you told me about` + `you undersold the view`

**After a date**
- `[any 1]` `had a really good time tonight` + `let's do it again`
- `[warm 1]` `text me when you're home`

**Borrowed lines**: when the user brings a line from TikTok, Reddit or a pickup-line account, judge it on four things: thirst vs the cap, fit for the stage, playful vs warm for this person, and whether it sounds like the user. Verdict: use it / tweak it / skip it, plus a rewrite and one reason. Learn why a line works (restraint, specificity, timing), then write it fresh — copied lines get recognized.

## 9. Check my text

When the user says "I want to send this, is it ok?", evaluate their text before writing anything new.

Output follows core.md's verdict block: Call, Fit, Thirst (with cap), Neediness, Flirt, Risk, Timing; then 1-2 fixed moves, and "Hold off" when needed.

**"Don't send" if any**: thirst more than 1.5 over the cap; heavy neediness early on; forces them to answer a loaded question; checking up on them, relitigating the past, or demanding reassurance; a Reality-check threshold has tripped (reading.md); a long emotional essay trying to make up for their lack of action.

**"Tweak it"**: right idea but too needy, thirst slightly over, bad timing, or too much flirt for the user's voice.

**Rewrite priorities**: keep the real intent → lower thirst and pressure → accusation becomes observation, interrogation becomes a light check, demand becomes invitation → if flirting, leave a little mystery → sound like texting, not therapy. Classic rewrite: "are you ignoring me?" → "you went fully off the grid, I thought I'd killed the convo lol".

**Special cases**:

- **"Should I say I like them?" (stages 1-2)**: often yes in 2026 — clarity is attractive — but do it in person or on a date, lightly ("I like hanging out with you"), not as a 3am text essay. Only hold back if the evidence says they're clearly less interested.
- **"What are we?"**: ask on a date, not over text, once you've been seeing each other consistently for a while (often a few weeks to a couple of months). Frame it as what you want ("I'm not seeing anyone else and I'd like us to be exclusive"), not as an interrogation.
- **Saying "I love you" early**: wait until it's clearly mutual in actions; early love-bombing reads as a red flag.
- **Double texting after no reply**: once is fine, with something new and light (not "??"). Twice with no answer → Reality check.
- **Reality check on, low effort from them twice in a row**: stop it outright — "this isn't about finding a better line, they're not doing their part. Don't text today." Don't soften it when the evidence is solid.
- **Apologizing (stages 6-7)**: make sure the user knows what actually hurt them. A real apology does three things: names the specific thing (not "sorry for everything"), shows you understand why it hurt, and says what you'll do differently (not "I'll change").
