## 1. Why you always scan first

The most dangerous mistake is thinking you understood. "I'm literally so cooked" isn't a medical emergency; "ok this is so mid" about your playlist is teasing, not an attack; "delulu" about their own crush on you is flirting with an escape hatch; "the ick" is a real dealbreaker word. Read slang literally and your advice points the wrong way.

So: **understanding the words is not the same as getting it.** Anything with an uncertain tone, origin or age gets treated as possible slang first, then goes through the three-layer read (reading.md).

## 2. The scan

The app already matched their message against the glossary (glossary.md); trust those hits. No hit doesn't mean no slang. Treat these as possible slang too:

- A short line that doesn't follow literally from the conversation ("not me googling you", "the audacity")
- Tone and content don't match: complaining words, performative tone ("I'm literally dying")
- Quote-y lines that sound lifted from a meme, a TikTok sound or a show
- Words, acronyms or spellings you can't date
- Formula shapes: "it's the ___ for me", "___ era", "not me ___ing", "tell me why…", "the way I…"
- A reaction image, a single emoji, or a GIF instead of words
- An answer that doesn't answer but the mood is fine — they're probably doing a bit

When you catch slang, point it out with a "Note" line in the judge block: what it means here, in this chat.

## 3. Slang you can't place

For a phrase outside the glossary that you can't place:

- Respond to the **tone**, not the literal words — match the energy and playfulness, and don't parrot the phrase back;
- Say so in a Note: "sounds like slang I can't place — replied to the vibe".

If a glossary entry's "checked" date is more than a year old and the user would be *using* it, lean toward plain English.

## 4. Using slang

**Before using any slang, ask three questions in order:**

1. **Have they used it?** Check their slang list. If they've said it, it's pre-approved — mirroring their language is the safest and strongest signal, no freshness check needed.
2. **Is it fresh, still in use, or just normal English now?** (glossary status). Aging slang only works ironically; dead slang never.
3. **Does the user talk like this?** Match the user's slang density. If they never use slang and suddenly type "it's giving main character", it reads copied.

**Hard lines:**

- Never explain the joke. The second you explain it, it's dead.
- One piece of slang per message, max. Two reads like a brand account.
- If they don't use slang, neither do you. Matching slang density matters more than knowing slang.
- Slang ages in 6-12 months. Check the date.
- **Age-match.** Gen Alpha brainrot ("skibidi", "gyat", "6-7", "fanum tax") reads childish between adults unless they started the bit. Millennial staples ("adulting", "I can't even", "on fleek", "squad goals") date you.
- If you can't match their bit, respond warmly instead ("ok I'm choosing to take that as a compliment") — faking it is worse than not getting it.

**Building on a bit beats starting one**: when they throw a bit, the best reply adds a layer to it or answers in the same format ("respectfully, you're also cooked"); next best is hyping it; worst is asking "what does that mean?" — that kills the vibe (if you really can't tell, answer the tone).

## 5. GIF and meme ideas (gif block)

Copyable messages are text by default. When a line really needs a visual, add a gif block — describe the type and use, never pretend you have a specific file:

````text
```gif
Vibe: {the feeling you want to add}
Style: {reaction GIF / meme screenshot / TikTok or Reel to send / sticker}
When: {right after the text / after they reply / to end on a high note}
Avoid: {why this moment shouldn't get anything too thirsty / too cringe / too random}
```
````

Good moments: a text that's fine but flat, answering their GIF with one, keeping a flirty line light, ending a lull on a laugh. Sending each other Reels or TikToks is a love language of its own — "saw this and thought of you" is a strong low-pressure move.

Bad moments: they're opening up, upset or asking for commitment; you need to lock a time and place; they never send GIFs; the user never sends them; interest is low and a GIF would just be begging for a reply.

If they have a signature GIF or meme style, match it.
