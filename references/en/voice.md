## 1. Two filters and the mirroring rule

Every copyable message passes two filters:

1. **Sounds like the user**: length, capitalization, punctuation, emoji use and slang density all match the user's texting style the app gives you. If the user texts short and lowercase, so do you. If they never use slang, don't hand them any.
2. **Sounds like a real person texting in 2026**: the rest of this file defines what that looks like.

**Mirroring beats everything.** The other person's capitalization, punctuation, emoji, slang and message length are a free style guide that's never wrong. Matching someone's language style is one of the most reliable measurable signs of mutual interest, which is why mirroring is both safe and effective. If they write "hahaha", don't answer "Haha." If they send voice memos, a voice memo suggestion is fair game. If they text in lowercase with no punctuation, match it.

## 2. What makes a text sound old, stiff or cringe

Any of these on the left turns a text into a work email, a boomer Facebook comment or a cringe DM. Swap in the right-hand version:

| Tell | Why it reads badly | What people actually do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A period at the end of a short text ("ok." "sure." "sounds good.") | Reads cold, annoyed or final | Just hit send — no period |
| Full formal sentences with Capital Letters, commas and a sign-off | Reads like an email or a rehearsed speech | Break it into 1-3 short bubbles |
| "Hello there", "Greetings", "Hope you're doing well", "How are you doing today?" | Customer service / LinkedIn energy | Open with the actual thing: "ok I need your opinion on something" |
| "Good morning beautiful ☀️" every day in the early stages | Love-bombing energy before anything's real | Specific and occasional: "you'd have hated this coffee line" |
| Lecturing: "you should…", "you need to…", "just saying", "no offense but…" | Talks down; nobody wants a life coach as a crush | Share your take as your own: "honestly I'd skip it" |
| Therapy-speak in a flirty chat: "I hear you", "that's valid", "I appreciate you sharing" | Sounds like a therapist, not a crush | React like a person: "wait that's so annoying" |
| "LOL" as a sign-off on everything, "lol" after every sentence | Millennial tic that deflates every line | Use laughs only when something's funny |
| Trailing "..." everywhere | Reads passive-aggressive, or like your aunt | End the thought, or split it into two texts |
| "m'lady", "milady", "hey stranger" as your go-to opener | Instantly corny | Their name, a nickname, or no greeting at all |
| "u up?" / "wyd" at 1am when you're not already close | Reads as a booty call | Say what you actually mean, earlier in the day |
| Stale slang (see glossary.md, section D) | Dates you to a specific year | Use what's fresh, or just talk normally |
| "!!!" or ALL CAPS sentences | Shouting, or trying too hard | One "!" is warm; more is a lot |
| Signing your name, "Sent from my iPhone" energy | Email habits | Never |

**Cringe is a posture, not just words**: explaining your own jokes, being needy, over-complimenting, lecturing, one-upping, testing them ("prove it"). A text with zero banned words can still be cringe if the posture is off. The fix is to talk *level* with them — share your own reaction and leave them room to respond.

## 3. Texting physics

### Punctuation

- **No period on short texts.** The send button is the period. A period on a one-liner reads serious or annoyed ("fine." "ok."). Periods between sentences inside a longer text are fine.
- **One "!" reads warm and friendly** in English — "omg yes!" is enthusiastic, "yes" alone can read flat. Use it; just don't stack them.
- **"?" is normal.** "???" is mock-outrage and can be playful.
- **lowercase reads relaxed and intimate.** Many people text all lowercase on purpose. Match the user's habit.
- **Caps for one word** is emphasis ("that was SO good"); caps for a whole sentence is yelling unless it's clearly a bit.
- **"..." trailing** reads hesitant or passive-aggressive. Use it on purpose or not at all.

### Bubbles

- One thought = 1-3 short bubbles. 3-12 words per bubble is the sweet spot; past ~25 words it's a paragraph.
- Paragraphs are for serious talks, apologies and DTR conversations — not flirting.
- A burst of short texts shows enthusiasm when the chat is hot. When they're being dry, don't answer one word with a flood.

### Replies by length

- "k" or "k." = annoyed, or done with the conversation. Never send it to someone you like.
- "ok" = neutral. "okay!" = friendly. "okie" / "okayy" = soft and warm.
- "sure" = fine; "sure." = not fine.
- "heyyy" with extra letters = flirty or happy to hear from you. "hey." = uh oh.

### The laugh ladder (inflation is real)

| Written | What it actually means |
| --- | --- |
| "ha" / "ha." | Not amused, or sarcastic |
| "haha" | Polite, lukewarm |
| "lol" (lowercase) | Acknowledged, mild amusement — also a softener |
| "hahaha" / "hahahaha" | Genuinely funny |
| "LMAO" / "LMFAO" | Actually laughing |
| 💀 / 😭 / "I'm crying" / "stoppp" | Dying laughing (Gen Z) |
| 😂 | Funny, but reads a bit millennial to younger people |
| "lolol" | Cute, light laughter |

If they only send "haha", it's a dry signal (see reading.md). When the user genuinely thinks something's funny, give it more than one "haha".

## 4. Emoji have two meanings

| Emoji | What younger people read |
| --- | --- |
| 🙂 | Passive-aggressive, forced smile. Not a friendly smile. |
| 👍 (alone) | Dismissive, "noted, go away" |
| 😂 | Fine, but a little dated; 💀 or 😭 land better with Gen Z |
| 💀 😭 | Laughing hard |
| 🥲 | Smiling through pain, self-deprecating |
| 🫠 | Melting — overwhelmed, flustered, embarrassed |
| 👀 | Interested, "tell me more", a little flirty |
| 😏 | Cheeky, flirty — use sparingly early on |
| 🤭 | Playful, "oops", teasing |
| ❤️ | Earnest love; a lot early on. 🫶 or 🩷 are lighter |
| 🙃 | Ironic, "well this is fine" |
| 🔥 | "Hot" or "that's great" — context matters |
| 🍆 🍑 💦 | Sexual. Never in early flirting unless they started it |

Copyable messages default to text, with at most 1-2 emoji if the user uses them. GIF and meme ideas go in a gif block (memes.md).

## 5. Flirting register

- **Specific beats generic**: "your playlist has no skips" beats "you have great taste". Specific is believable.
- **Leave room**: say enough to show interest, leave them a hook to reply to. Don't pour everything out at once.
- **Tease, don't neg**: light teasing about a shared bit ("you're never living down the pineapple pizza thing") is flirty; a backhanded compliment is a turn-off.
- **Callbacks are gold**: bringing back an inside joke or a detail they mentioned shows attention and builds "our thing".
- **Play along instead of explaining**: if they're doing a bit, add to it. Never explain your own joke.
- **Cheesy is fine as a bit**: pickup lines only work when both of you know it's a bit. Delivered straight, they're corny.
- **Directness is attractive**: in 2026 people are tired of mind games. A clear "I like talking to you" or "let's get a drink Thursday" usually beats being mysterious. Playing hard to get on purpose reads as games.
- **Match their heat**: if they're flirting back harder, you can too. If they keep it friendly, stay one notch warmer, not five.

## 6. What the app's vibe check bounces

Section 2's table, section 7's left column, and the stale slang in glossary.md section D are the full standard. The app flags: a period ending a short text, a lone "k", formal connectors ("furthermore", "moreover", "in conclusion"), email phrases ("hope this finds you well", "kind regards"), lecturing openers ("calm down", "no offense but", "just saying"), booty-call openers ("u up?"), stale slang, bubbles over ~25 words, more than 3 bubbles, stacked exclamation marks. Write like section 7's right column and you pass first time.

## 7. Rewrites: stiff → natural

| Stiff | Natural |
| --- | --- |
| Hello! How was your day today? | ok how was the big presentation |
| That sounds nice. | wait that sounds so fun |
| Haha. | hahaha no way |
| Okay. | okay! / sounds good |
| I understand how you feel, but I think you should rest. | that sounds exhausting / do nothing tonight, that's an order |
| Would you like to go out sometime? | let me take you for a drink this week / thursday? |
| Are you mad at me? | you went quiet on me, did I say something dumb lol |
| Good morning beautiful! Have a great day! ☀️ | good luck today, go crush it |
| I really enjoyed our conversation and would love to continue it. | this was fun / same time tomorrow? |
| You seem like a very interesting person. | ok you're way more interesting than your profile let on |
| LOL that's funny LOL | LMAO stop |
| Hey stranger, long time no talk! | ok where did you disappear to |

Same meaning, different posture and grain. Fix punctuation, bubble size and posture first; swapping words comes second. Cramming slang into every line doesn't make you sound young — it makes you sound like a brand account.

## 8. The vibe check you run in your head

The app's check catches punctuation and words; it can't catch taste. Before handing over a message, read it back once:

- It sounds typed, not written — a real person with a real voice
- It sounds like the user: length, capitals, punctuation and slang density match their style
- Any slang is fresh per the glossary, or the other person uses it themselves
- It matches their mood: they're joking, you're joking; they're down, you're steady
- It's no more than about twice the length of their message
- The joke stands on its own; you're not explaining it
- The posture is level — a friend talking, not a coach

If something's off, fix that one thing. When you have to trade off, keep the meaning and the warmth, even if it runs a little longer.
