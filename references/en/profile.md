# Reading people: screenshots, socials and the dossier

Two jobs: how to read a person from screenshots and their socials, and what goes into the dossier.

---

## 1. Reading screenshots

- Look properly before judging. Timestamps, names, small text, reactions, read receipts — all evidence. If you can't read it, say so and ask for a clearer screenshot rather than guessing.
- Work out whose bubbles are whose. iMessage: the user is usually blue (or green for SMS) on the right, the other person grey on the left. WhatsApp: the user is green on the right. Instagram DMs: the user is on the right (purple/blue gradient). Dating apps vary — right side is usually the user. If you can't tell, infer from content, or say you can't.
- Chat screenshots: focus on actions — who started this thread, did they pick up the ball, did they ask back, is there a plan with a specific time and place, did they follow through, any cooling signs. Tapbacks/reactions ("Loved", "Laughed at", "Emphasized") count as low-effort engagement; a reaction instead of a reply to a question is a mild dodge.
- "Read 2:14 PM" with no reply matters only against their baseline.
- Long screenshots or several at once: read every one, in order. Don't skim to the "key" one.
- Only claim what's visible; mark inferences.

## 2. Reading socials (Instagram, TikTok, dating profiles)

A profile is a curated front, not the whole person. Say what you see and back it with evidence.

### What they show

| Type | Typical content | Opening angle |
|------|----------|----------|
| Everyday life | Food, commute, small moments | Pick up a specific detail |
| Taste | Cafés, shows, music, outfits, travel | Compliment a specific choice |
| Achievements | Work, school, fitness, certificates | Genuine props, no groveling |
| Feelings | Late-night captions, lyrics | Meet the feeling before flirting |
| Social life | Parties, friends, events | Show your own life; don't interrogate |
| Low-key / private | Rarely posts | Keep thirst low, don't pry, let the chat do the work |

Back any read with evidence and a confidence. "Easy to get" / "hard to get" comes from chat behavior, not from a photo or an outfit.

### Dating profiles

Prompts are gifts: Hinge prompts, Bumble badges and bios tell you exactly what to open with. The best opener picks one specific thing and adds a take or a question ("bold claim about your tacos, I need proof"). "Hey" and compliments on looks alone get ignored.

### Looks and style

Note how they show themselves (selfies / photos by others / group shots / faceless), style (streetwear, minimal, preppy, outdoorsy, vintage…), and the mood of their photos. Use: specific compliments instead of "you're pretty", picking date spots and gifts, helping the user show a matching side of themselves.

### Interactions and player signals

Comments: who shows up (friends, coworkers, admirers), how they reply (warm, dry, playful, flirty), whether they flirt publicly. One person's joke in the comments isn't a fact. Curated-persona, public-flirt, "posting at you", holiday-flirting and rotation patterns and their tests are in reading.md section 4.

### Don't read romance into aesthetics

Deep captions, moody photos and playful comments don't mean they're into the user. Whether they're interested comes back to actions: do they DM, ask back, add detail, suggest plans, turn public flirting into private plans, choose the user over others?

### Three kinds of topics

Light (safe openers), deeper (after a few exchanges), and not yet (salary, body, exes, their location, heavy looks commentary) — the last group loses points fast.

When the user sends profile screenshots in Reply mode, the three moves are openers: Steady (a specific detail), Flirt (curiosity or a playful contrast), Wildcard (show a matching side of the user's own life).

## 3. What goes in the dossier

The dossier is the app's long-term memory of concrete facts about them, each with a source. Concrete and reusable only: "sensitive" doesn't go in; "sends '?' if I don't reply in 15 minutes" does.

| Slot | What | Example |
|------|--------|------|
| Basics | Birthday, city, job, school, age, zodiac/MBTI (only if they said it) | Birthday: Oct 12 |
| Likes | Specific things that light them up | Loves live jazz |
| Dislikes | Things that kill the mood, touchy topics | Hates talking about work on weekends |
| Food | Allergies, restrictions, favorites | Vegetarian |
| Routine | Schedule, habits, standing plans | Climbs Wednesday nights |
| Catchphrases | Their pet phrases and texting habits | Says "stoppp" when flattered |
| Wishlist | Small things they want to try | Wants to try pottery |
| Plans | Time-bound things: an event on a date, a busy week | Exam next Friday |
| Promises | Things the two of them agreed on | Said we'd try the taco truck |
| Subtext | Their personal "says A, means B" | "I'm fine" = ask me again |

Rules:

- Only what they said, did, or what's visible. Things the user reports count too, with the source. Guesses don't go in.
- Plans expire: with a date, after that date; with "tomorrow" or "next week", computed from the screenshot's date.
- When facts conflict, the newer one wins for the same kind of fact (a corrected birthday); keep the old one on file.
- Inconsistencies: record facts and counts, don't label someone a liar.
